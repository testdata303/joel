# JOEL app - project notes

**NO EM DASHES. EVER.** Not in copy, headings, meta tags, schema, code comments, or docs. Use a comma, colon, period, or parentheses.

**Marketing-site work: start at `docs/marketing/README.md` - then `docs/marketing/NUMBERLINE.md` (operating manual) and `docs/marketing/CONTENT_BIBLE.md` (voice & editorial standards) - before creating or editing any page. The permanent site is intentionally small (Home, How it works & features, Pricing, About, Early access, Sign in); every other marketing page follows `docs/marketing/MARKETING.md`: disposable campaign pages under `/go/`, durable comparison pages under `/compare/` (e.g. /compare/google-voice), and owner-approved root feature pages (/slack, /whatsapp). Campaign pages never appear in nav or footer; feature pages sit in the footer Product column and comparison pages in the footer Compare column (never the nav).**

HTML entry: `Extension Detail.html`. React + Babel app loaded from `app/*.jsx`, styles in `assets/*.css`.

## Greeting / AI-voice requirement (per product owner)
- The **AI voice** used for greetings is taken from **Quick Setup** (the business-name pronunciation step).
- The **phonetic spelling** captured in Quick Setup pre-populates the greeting **text** on the Greetings screen (GreetingRow "System default" / "AI voice").
- When a greeting is **saved**, it is rendered **once to an audio file** - the system does NOT run AI text-to-speech on every call. Treat saved greetings as static audio.
- Quick Setup main-greeting step has a **"Use default greeting instead"** toggle: it drops the business name from the greeting. Choosing it should set the main greeting to **System default** on the Greetings settings screen.

## Quick Setup flow (app/quick-setup.jsx)
Steps: intro → name (business name + main greeting combined) → forward → voicemail → hold (music) → ready (review) → texts (Finished).
- Two-number convention: an Active number (green) usable immediately + a Porting number (amber) when `numberPending`.
- "Set up manually instead" dismisses Quick Setup (warns first, then removes it from the sidebar; owner-only).

## Branded "function design" (marketing site)
The canonical way to show how Numberline works is the **function list** born on the homepage hero: one row per function - colored icon tile + bold name + outcome chip, rows NEVER wrap to two lines. Component (`.hsteps/.hstep`) + the function color palette (`.fn-number` blue, `.fn-ext` soft yellow, `.fn-fwd` purple, `.fn-vm` green, `.fn-sms` cyan, `.fn-ai` pink) live in `assets/site.css`. Use these exact colors anywhere a function appears (icons, chips, diagram destinations) on ANY page - e.g. anything integrations/AI-related is always pink, voicemail always green.

**Sample Setup card** (`.sample-setup`, specimen: `docs/marketing/components/sample-setup-tree.html`) is the second signature component: question → sample setup → explanation on every major page. White card, light header + "Sample setup" tag, business number row on top, tree connectors down to function-colored destination rows with status chips. The setup varies per page; the component's visual language never does. Static today, but keep rows as discrete nodes so it can become interactive later.

## Campaign pages (/go/*, templates in docs/marketing/templates/campaigns/) - copy + template conventions
- Mobile-first positioning: destinations are people’s mobiles (owner’s mobile, assistant’s mobile, on-call mobile), the existing business/practice phone, or an outside answering provider - never "front desk", "operator", "office line", "desk phone", or "call center" as product destinations (real-life front-desk mentions in pain copy are OK; comparison rows may discuss front-desk-phone-only setups). Callbacks come from the business number via the mobile app.
- H1 formula: "The [vertical] phone system for [calls], [texts], &amp; follow-up." Headings (h1/h2/h3) use "&amp;" instead of "and" site-wide; body copy, meta, schema, and aria-labels keep "and". Position = organization, not missed calls: the business owns calls, texts, voicemails, callbacks, and follow-up. Missed-call language only in voicemail/transcript, follow-up-alert, after-hours, and comparison content.
- Hero bullets order: 1) business line for website/profile/materials, 2) forwarding to the right people, 3) texts/voicemails/follow-up stay with the business.
- Vertical campaign pages have NO separate "Example setup" section - the hero shot is the only setup mockup. Mockup cards (hero .shot-top) use a light product-UI header: bg #fff, border-bottom #E5E7EB, text #1F2329 - never pure black; the CTA button stays the strongest dark element. The "No per-user pricing" stamp is #25272E stroke/text on #fff, 3px border, shadow 0 8px 24px rgba(19,19,22,.10).
- Hero-shot card title: "Sample [vertical] phone setup" (never a fictional business name as the headline). Greeting caption is "Callers hear your greeting" (no fake day/time captions). Bottom rows of the hero shot: "Texts / Voicemails - Sent to shared inbox, email, or Slack" and "After hours - Forward to a third-party answering service" (short, no line-breaking sentences).
- After-hours routing in hero shots: a STANDARD route row (fn-ai moon icon), never a separate strip (the pink .shot-ah strip is retired). Row title "After hours"; copy = "Forward [vertical call type] to your own answering provider or the third-party AI answering service you choose." Law firms: prefer "third-party answering provider", keep AI low-key. Never "Answering services" or "AI answering" as a label (Numberline does not provide answering); never "send any option", "front desk swamped", "switch it off whenever you like", or Numberline-owned AI.

- HARD RULE, enforced globally in assets/canon.css: EVERY dial-key visual (.fic.key, .prow .k, .menu-ex .pk, and any future key tile) renders as a keycap with the tiny "press"/"dial" label, so menu options never read as step numbers 1-2-3.
- Comparison table (`.cmp`, styles in `assets/canon.css`, canonical DATA SOURCE `docs/marketing/components/comparison-table.html`): comparison claims are product data, not page copy - they are updated in the canonical comparison source, verified against official sources (verification metadata lives in the specimen), and inherited everywhere. Polarity law: alternatives carry ONLY red ✕ `.gap` chips (qualified, durable downsides - never absolutes like "No integrations" or "They hold your number"); Numberline rows carry ONLY green ✓ `.has` chips, 4-6 selected per page from the approved benefit pool. Chips are 3-7 words and never wrap. Column heading is "What to know". A page chooses WHICH rows appear (3-4 alternatives + Numberline); it never rewrites a row.
- Meta description formula: vertical + phone system/business line + calls + texts + voicemails or transcripts + follow-up.
- Root-relative links for all pages (/go/..., /how-it-works, /early-access.html); asset links stay ../ relative. Campaign pages never appear in the nav or footer; the footer is Product + Compare + Company columns.
- Pre-launch: never "in minutes", "take calls today", "free porting", instant/temporary numbers, "month-to-month", "cancel anytime". Avoid "one number" phrasing unless the sentence says more numbers can be added. CTAs: "Get early access" primary, "See how it works" secondary. **CTA row order is LAW: secondary ("See how it works") on the LEFT, primary ("Get early access") on the RIGHT, and the primary always carries `btn-arrow` + `<span class="btn-guillemet">&#187;</span>`. Heroes with CTAs get the `.hero-bullets` checkmark list below the acts row (canonical bullet order above).** Slack is optional-only outside consultants; AI is always a third-party answering service, never Numberline's own. The feature/nav LABEL for this capability is **"Using third-party AI services"** (never "Third-party AI services", "Third-party AI", or "AI services" as a label - it is a capability, not a category of software); prose keeps "third-party AI answering service".
