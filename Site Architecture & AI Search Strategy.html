Marketing site architecture & AI search strategy

Numberline · Growth engineering memo · July 2026

Goal: an information architecture that ships the early-access site as-is today and absorbs thousands of pages over five years without a reorganization. The design principle throughout: the nav never scales — hubs scale. Navigation and footer point at a small, fixed set of hub pages; every future page is a spoke under one of them.

1Navigation

Keep the top nav at five items, permanently. It already links three of the four hubs it will ever need — add Learn and stop.

How it works · Features · Pricing · Learn ← add · Sign in · [Get early access]
  • Never put industries in the nav. At 8 pages a dropdown looks thin; at 80 it's unusable. Industries live in the footer, on the homepage, and on the /for/ hub.
  • Features points at features.html today and at the /features/ hub when individual feature pages exist — same nav item, no change ever needed.
  • Post-launch additions (Integrations, Compare) also enter as footer hubs, not nav items. The nav is a product statement, not a sitemap.

  • Two levels, maximum. /hub/slug, never deeper. Categorization beyond that happens on hub pages, so pages can be re-grouped editorially without URL changes.
  • Keep /for/. It's already live, it reads as natural language ("Numberline for plumbers"), and it flexes across industries and audience stages where /industries/ can't.
  • One topic = one URL, forever. A URL is a promise to search engines and AI indexes. If a page's angle changes, rewrite the content in place; never fork a near-duplicate slug.
  • Drop .html now. Netlify's "pretty URLs" serves /for/plumbers from for/plumbers.html; add .html→extensionless 301s in _redirects and a canonical tag on every page. Pre-launch is the only free moment to do this.
  • Slugs: lowercase, hyphenated, no dates, no stop-words unless the phrase needs them (personal-cell-vs-business-phone is fine).

5Internal linking

The knowledge graph comes from standardized blocks, not ad-hoc links. Every spoke page carries three:

  1. Breadcrumb to its hub (Learn → Business phone number) — with BreadcrumbList schema.
  2. In-body contextual links where a feature, number type, or industry is mentioned, using descriptive anchors that match the target's H1 ("shared business texting", not "learn more").
  3. A "Related" block before the footer, cross-type by rule:
    • Industry pages → 3 features that vertical leans on + 2 learn guides + the relevant number type.
    • Learn pages → 2–3 features + 1–2 industries where the answer applies + 1 sibling guide.
    • Feature pages → 2–3 industries that showcase it + 1–2 guides + hub.
    • Number pages → the pillar business-phone-number guide + 2 features + 1 industry.

Publish rule: no page ships with fewer than 3 inbound links — add it to its hub index and to 2 related pages in the same commit. The three pillar guides (business phone number, virtual phone system, small business phone system) act as connective tissue and should eventually link to nearly everything.

8AI search & SEO scalability

  • One canonical entity definition. The footer paragraph is Numberline's machine-readable identity. Use it verbatim on every page, mirror it in Organization schema and an llms.txt at the root. Consistency across hundreds of pages is what teaches AI models what Numberline is.
  • Answer-first pages. Every spoke page opens with a 40–60-word direct answer under the H1 that an AI engine can lift whole. Structure the rest for extraction: numbered steps, tables, definition-style headings that are real questions.
  • FAQ blocks with schema. 3–5 genuinely asked questions per page, marked up as FAQPage; plus BreadcrumbList everywhere. No schema types you can't honestly claim (no fabricated reviews or ratings pre-launch).
  • Depth beats breadth during beta. Publish only pages with unique substance — a thin 300-word vertical page hurts the domain's authority-per-page average that AI retrieval implicitly scores. 30 excellent pages now outperform 100 templated ones.
  • Hubs are the crawl surface. Every page ≤2 clicks from home via its hub; segment sitemap.xml per hub so index coverage per category is measurable.
  • Terminology discipline. The existing copy conventions (mobile-first destinations, third-party AI answering, no enterprise language) are also entity hygiene — AI engines learn the product's shape from consistent phrasing. Keep enforcing them mechanically.
  • Measure AI referrals now. Tag traffic from AI-assistant referrers/user-agents in analytics from day one, so you have a baseline before the channel matters.
  • Comparisons stay dark until launch — but write them during beta and hold them, so /compare/ can ship as a complete hub the week you launch instead of dripping out signals for months.
