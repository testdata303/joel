Vanity numbers, explained

ABC
How the word works

Every keypad digit carries letters (2 is ABC, 3 is DEF...). Dialing the letters dials the digits, so 1-800-555-CAKE is just a phone number wearing a word.

800
Toll-free vanity

The classic. 800 is the most recognized and the scarcest; 833, 844, 855, 866, 877, and 888 work identically and have far more words still available.

617
Local vanity

Possible when the digits line up in your area code, and rarer. Right when you want the word and the local signal together.

555
The honest alternative

If your word is taken, an easy-to-say repeating number can be just as memorable. The goal is a number people can repeat back, not the word itself.

Vanity number questions

What is a vanity phone number?
A number that spells a word with the keypad's letters, like 1-800-555-CAKE. Dialing the letters dials the digits; the word just makes it memorable.
Do vanity numbers have to be toll-free?
No, but most are: toll-free prefixes have the most available combinations. A local vanity number is possible when the digits line up, but far rarer.
What if my word is taken?
Try the same word on a newer prefix like 833 or 844, a shorter or adjacent word, or an easy-to-say repeating number. Memorability is the goal, not the exact word.
Can customers text a vanity number?
Yes. Texts land in a shared inbox your team can see and answer, and replies come from the vanity number.
Is 800 better than 833 or 844?
They work identically. 800 is the oldest and most recognized, and the scarcest; the newer prefixes carry the same toll-free signal with much better availability.
